# Alert Bug — motion and position

The interactivity alert on **layer 0**: it sits on top of the video, in the bottom-right
corner, over the Globo's own mark. A bottom-right shade is laid behind it for contrast.
This page is the spec for how it comes in, stays, leaves, and where it goes.

## Where it sits

The bug goes **on top of the Globo's watermark**. Its margins are measured from the
Globo's mark — the sphere — and not from the whole set of components, so the halo and the
text hang outside the margin and never move the sphere.

The kit draws in its own 1280×720 frame (`--dimension-size-semantic-alert-bug-mark` and
`-halo`); the FHD numbers are the same layout at 1.5×. The two margins are the same fraction
of the frame at both sizes, so the kit holds them as percentages: **6.1458 % to the right, 5.1852 % to the bottom**. `AlertBugAnchor` does it;
it fills the 16:9 frame and puts its child there.

Due to technical limits in how the Bug is delivered in the video graphics layer
(watermarks and lit versions), a fine adjustment of the position may still be needed on a
given transmission. Change it on `AlertBugAnchor`, never on the bug.

## Contrast behind it

The shade behind the bug is not part of the bug: it is the `alert` screen model of
`ScreenOverlay` (`bottom-right`), already in the kit — see **Camadas**. Stack it between
the picture and the anchor:

```tsx
<div style={{ position: 'relative' /* the whole 16:9 frame */ }}>
  <video … />
  <ScreenOverlay model="alert" />
  <AlertBugAnchor>
    <AlertBug bugStyle="transmission" src={globoMark} motion="cycle" />
  </AlertBugAnchor>
</div>
```

## Motion

Three layers, all of them animated together, with the same easing (the kit's spring as a
cubic-bezier) and the kit's 800 ms.

<table>
  <thead>
    <tr><th>Layer</th><th>Entrance (0 → 100 %)</th><th>Exit (100 → 0 %)</th></tr>
  </thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr>
      <td><b>Bug</b> (the sphere)</td>
      <td>focuses in: opacity 0 → 100, scale 0.9 → 1</td>
      <td>scale 1 → 0.9, opacity 100 → 0</td>
    </tr>
    <tr>
      <td><b>Halo</b> (the focus ring's band)</td>
      <td>born the size of the sphere, opens to its final size, opacity 0 → 100, scale 0.9 → 1</td>
      <td>scale 1 → 0.9, opacity 100 → 0</td>
    </tr>
    <tr>
      <td><b>Text</b> ("Conteúdo interativo")</td>
      <td>behind the bug; rises out of it, bottom to top, opacity 0 → 100</td>
      <td>drops back behind the bug, opacity 100 → 0</td>
    </tr>
  </tbody>
</table>

- The bug and the halo "rise" at the same moment.
- The halo **spins for as long as the bug is on screen**, one turn per
  `--motion-semantic-focus-cycle-duration` (12 s). It is the focus ring turning, not a
  loading indicator.
- The bug **stays 8 s**, then leaves on its own. `onExited` fires when the exit is done and
  the bug is no longer drawn (its box stays, so nothing around it moves).
- The halo is the focus ring's band with the broadcast gradient —
  `linear-gradient(224deg, …)` through the *noite*, *dia* and *tarde* tokens. Its pearl stop
  is `EEDDFE`; the kit has no token for it, so it uses `text-primary` until one is added.

`motion` takes `enter` (800 ms and stay), `stay` (the settled composition), `exit` (800 ms)
and `cycle` (all of it: enter, 8 s, exit). Without `motion` the bug is the still mark.

### Reduced motion

With `prefers-reduced-motion`, or `reducedMotion` on the component, the bug only
**dissolves** in and out: no scale, no rise, no drop, no spin.

## Chrome 68 and Safari 16

The whole motion is CSS and only moves `transform` and `opacity`, so it stays on the GPU of
an old TV. What it avoids on purpose: `linear()` easing, `inset`, `gap`, `aspect-ratio`,
`:is()`, `@property`, `translate`/`scale`/`rotate` as properties, `conic-gradient`,
`min()`/`max()`/`clamp()`, `filter: blur`, and animating `width`, `height` or the gradient.
The spin is `rotate()` on its own element. The table of what each feature needs is the
comment above the motion rules in `ui-kit.css`.
