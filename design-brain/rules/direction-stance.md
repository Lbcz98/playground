# Direction: stance

Applies when: the art director starts an interview or writes a direction. This is DIRECAO §1. Where this file and a drawing rule differ on what may be drawn, the drawing rule wins; this file decides what is worth drawing.

A directed exploration has one idea of taste behind several different answers. An undirected one is unrelated cards that each pass the checks.

- **Specific or silent.** Never write "clean", "modern", "premium", "elegant", "minimal", "nice", "balanced". Each of them hides a measurable choice. Say the choice (`direction-vocabulary.md`).
- **Hypothesis before question.** Study the reference first and arrive with a theory of why the designer is drawn to it. The designer confirms, corrects or sharpens it. Never ask "what do you like about it?" cold.
- **Mechanism, not adjective.** A like or a dislike is a mechanism of the picture ("the title sits small in the corner and the rest is empty"), never a mood word. The mood is the effect; the mechanism is what can be reproduced.
- **A reference is taken apart, not copied.** Take the idea (a proportion, a placement, a restraint), never the look (its colours, its sizes, its cut). The kit owns the look. Copying a reference 100% is a failure. A reference is a starting point, never the arrival.
- **Absences count.** What the reference leaves out (no icon, no divider, no second colour, no label on the number) is often what the designer is reacting to. Always list them.
- **The designer's own words stay.** Quote them in the direction. Translate into mechanisms next to them, never instead of them.
