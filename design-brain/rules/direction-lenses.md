# Direction: ten lenses

Applies when: the art director reads a reference in interview mode. This is DIRECAO §2.

Look at the image small first (squint), then large. Write one line per lens, with a number or a named choice. A lens with nothing to say is written "none" (that is an absence, and it counts).

1. **Void and mass.** What share of the surface is empty? Is the empty area one block or scattered? Where does the mass sit (a corner, a band, the centre)? Write it as a ratio ("about 9 parts empty to 1 part text").
2. **Anchor and axis.** What is read first, and why (size, contrast, isolation, position)? Is there one left axis or several? Where does the eye enter and where does it leave?
3. **Type.** Size contrast between the biggest and the smallest text (as a multiple); weights used; case; tracking; line length and leading; where the baseline of the small text sits against the big one; whether the text is optically placed.
4. **Colour.** How many hues; which one carries meaning; saturation and value range; what colour does that neutral could not.
5. **Light and depth.** Is there a light source? A gradient, and which way it falls; glow, grain, blur, scrim; whether depth comes from a surface change or from effects.
6. **Image.** Subject, crop, scale, how much of the card it takes; where the calm area of the image is and what sits on it.
7. **Rhythm and spacing.** The base module; margins as a share of the width; whether gaps between groups are one value or many; how breathing changes between groups.
8. **Shape.** Radii (one family or several); cuts, notches, perforations, outlines; icons as drawn shapes or as glyphs.
9. **Behaviour implied.** What the picture suggests it does; which part looks selectable; whether the focus would be a ring, a fill or a lift.
10. **Temperature and tension.** In one phrase, what the picture feels like and what keeps it from being flat. If the answer is "nothing", the picture is only quiet, and the designer is probably drawn by something else.

The output of a reading is a short table (lens, finding) and then **the theory**: two or three sentences naming the mechanisms most likely to be what the designer is drawn to, ordered by confidence, plus one thing that could be misread.
