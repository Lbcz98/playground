# Repeated items

Applies when: the option is interactive, the direction names `esqueleto-fixo` or `rotulo-inequivoco`, or the content is a row of days, hours, steps or chips.

1. **4 at most.** A row of repeatable items (days, hours, steps, chips) has 4 items by default. "Say more with less": when the full set will not fit, reframe it (the next 3 days, not the 7 of the week) instead of shrinking type, ring or spacing. A row of identical items counts as one piece in the 4-piece budget, but never as a licence for more than 4. If more items would really serve the viewer, do not build them: state the intent, explain why, and let the designer decide. After the designer says yes, mark the row `data-items-ok="<reason>"`. The layout check prints `itens`.
2. **Reduce items, not richness.** Obeying the 4-item rule must not make the option poorer. Cutting a row from 7 to 4 keeps the parent's style, composition and prominent graphic; it does not swap them for a plainer layout. A full chart stays a full chart (its curve whole and legible, with 4 labels on the axis, even when the chart is only informative and has no key). The group of weather data stays one group near the selector. The weather graphic keeps its prominence. A place name is never lost in the layout. An option richer than the one it replaces in one respect and poorer in another is a regression.
3. **Cause before symptom.** Before reporting small text or a tight card, ask whether the item count is the cause. If so, one finding, "itens demais", and do not also report the small text as a separate Critical. Never propose a smaller ring, tighter spacing or smaller type to make room for more items.
4. A fixed skeleton across variants: when several items or phases share a card, fix title position, margins, radius and subtitle and change only the data zone, at most three things per item. Detail: `conceitos/esqueleto-fixo`. *Why: what does not change lets the person see what does.*

The piece budget is 4: a piece is a group of text, a graphic or a control. More than 4 pieces → cut, do not shrink. Extra data goes behind the interaction, not on the resting card.

Don't: more than 4 repeated items without the designer's decision; shrinking the ring or the type to fit the week.
